Problem Statement

1

Information overload

Lecture content is distributed across long PDF files, making the right explanation difficult to locate when students need it.

2

Slow manual searching

Keyword search and page-by-page reading consume revision time and can miss conceptually related passages.

3

Ungrounded AI answers

Generic assistants may answer beyond approved course material without showing the lecture source behind the response.

Need: fast, source-grounded answers drawn from the student's own lecture notes.

Project Objectives

01

Preserve lecture evidence

Ingest PDF lecture notes while retaining source and page metadata for traceability.

02

Retrieve relevant passages

Combine dense semantic search with BM25 keyword matching to improve evidence discovery.

03

Generate grounded answers

Use retrieved context to produce answers with citations and transparent source snippets.

04

Evaluate the complete system

Assess retrieval, RAGAS answer quality, functionality, and practical response performance.

Project Scope and Boundaries

A focused lecture-note assistant—not a general learning-management platform

In scope

  • Text-readable PDF upload and extraction
  • Sentence-aware chunking with metadata
  • MiniLM embeddings and FAISS indexing
  • BM25 + dense hybrid retrieval with RRF
  • Gemini, OpenRouter, and local LLM generation
  • Cited answers, source snippets, web UI, and RAGAS evaluation

Out of scope

  • Non-PDF learning sources and scanned-PDF OCR
  • Automatic marking or student grading
  • Course-authoring and content-management tools
  • Institution-wide LMS integration
  • Claims of universal subject expertise
  • Replacement of lecturers or human academic support

Literature Review — Top Three Studies

Rank 01 · Educational QA

Alawwad et al. (2025)

Method: Fine-tuned Llama-2 with semantic retrieval for textual textbook QA.

Finding: 84.24% test accuracy on textual TQA.

Limitation: Text only; diagrams and visual evidence are excluded.

Project relevance: Closest evidence for RAG over structured lesson content.

Pattern Recognition · doi:10.1016/j.patcog.2024.111332

Rank 02 · Hybrid retrieval

Hu et al. (2025)

Method: Multimodal parsing with sparse-dense hybrid retrieval and LLM generation.

Finding: Reported correctness improved by 20.1% under the study setup.

Limitation: Real-time retrieval at document scale still needs optimization.

Project relevance: Supports combining BM25 and dense retrieval.

IEEE Access · doi:10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3544408

Rank 03 · Higher education

Neumann et al. (2024)

Method: GPT-4 RAG chatbot in Moodle using LangChain and Weaviate.

Finding: 88% response accuracy with positive student acceptance.

Limitation: Human tutors remained preferred; automated fact-checking was weak.

Project relevance: Demonstrates RAG as complementary academic support.

IEEE Transactions on Education · doi:10.1109/TE.2024.3467912

Research Gap and Project Contribution

Research gap

Existing studies show useful RAG retrieval and generation, but practical lecture-note assistants still need transparent source traceability, hybrid retrieval, deployment choice, and integrated evaluation.

Project contribution

This project combines PDF metadata, BM25 + FAISS retrieval, configurable cloud or local LLMs, cited answers, and RAGAS evaluation in one usable workflow.

Contribution is framed as an implementation response—not a claim of universal research novelty.

Limitations & Future Work

Current constraints and paths for extension

Current Limitations

  • Text-based PDFs only — no OCR for scanned documents
  • Single-server deployment without horizontal scaling
  • No multi-user authentication or session management
  • English-language lecture notes only

Future Enhancements

  • OCR support for image-based and scanned PDFs
  • Hybrid retrieval combining BM25 and dense vectors
  • Multi-user authentication with role-based access
  • Multilingual support for diverse materials
